\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}
\begin{quotation}
\noindent TrafficFlowBench asks for four coupled answers on ten synthetic but physically calibrated freeway corridors:
reconstruct masked speed and flow, forecast which links will be queued over the next 30 minutes, keep the
reconstruction consistent with the fundamental diagram and vehicle conservation, and recover path-level demand
from link counts. This report describes the pipeline behind our two final submissions. State is reconstructed by
gradient-boosted trees that learn the \emph{residual} of same-day temporal interpolation from 92 features of the
masked data. The predictions are reconciled with a predicted density, corrected on the free-flow branch by spatial
covariance and low-rank factor models, averaged over three independently sampled fits, and recentred per link on
short temporal gaps where a bias test shows the same-day interpolant to be unbiased. Queues are forecast from exactly
twelve history slots: onsets by a joint-mask analogue that maximizes empirical expected intersection over union across
complete training outcomes, and ongoing queues by a first-crossing classifier combined with a trajectory-analogue
library through an expected-IoU decoder. Demand is the exact non-negative least-squares solution on each split's own
counts and prior. Because the four tasks are scored on disjoint rows, the composite score is exactly additive, and we
use our evaluated submissions as a controlled ablation that identifies the score of each component. State
reconstruction and onset forecasting carry most of the improvement over the reference baselines. Every state change
selected on held-out training months improved the evaluated score, whereas only half of the queue changes did. We
also document more than thirty rejected approaches, including Godunov, Newell and diagram-inversion reconstructions,
all of which lost to temporal interpolation on held-out months.
\end{quotation}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} traffic state estimation; kinematic wave theory; queue forecasting; analogue forecasting;
gradient boosting; origin-destination estimation; reproducibility.
